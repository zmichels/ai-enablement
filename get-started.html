Choose your assistant

For work, start with your employer’s approved-tool directory or help desk. Ask what you already have, what needs a license or training, and what information you may use. Follow that tool’s work-account setup instructions.

Already have access? Go straight to your first prompt.

Prompt basics

Fictional practice task:

I’m helping organize a made-up neighborhood book swap. Write a friendly invitation under 100 words. Leave placeholders for the date and location, and give me two subject lines. No corporate pep talk, please.

Then ask: “Warmer. Half as long.” Check the details and refine.

Explore · Gemini Notebook

Turn sources into understanding.

Meet Gemini Notebook (formerly NotebookLM): a place to ask questions of your sources, follow citations, or try a study guide, mind map or Audio Overview. Google’s introduction

  1. Pick a topic.
  2. Add two or three trusted public sources.
  3. Ask a question. Open a citation. Check the answer.
Get my bearings
I’m new to this topic. Using these sources, explain the five things I should understand first. Keep the jargon to a minimum, cite the relevant passages, and flag anything the sources don’t answer.

Untangle a disagreement
Where do these sources agree and differ? Could their dates or intended audiences explain the difference? Show me the passages. Don’t smooth over a real disagreement.

Teach it back
Help me explain this in five minutes to someone new. Give me an outline, an everyday analogy and three questions to check understanding. Separate source findings from your teaching ideas.
