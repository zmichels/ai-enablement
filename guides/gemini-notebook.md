# Make friends with your reading pile

[Back to getting started](../get-started.html)

Gemini Notebook (formerly NotebookLM) is a useful place to explore a set of sources together. Add material you are allowed to use, ask questions, and follow citations back to the source. It can also make study guides, mind maps and Audio Overviews. Think of it as a reading companion with the books open. [Google's introduction](https://support.google.com/gemininotebook/answer/16164461?hl=en)

## A small first adventure

Pick something you actually want to understand: composting, local history, a public equipment manual, or a topic for a community class. Start with two or three public sources you trust. Keep private work documents out of this practice run.

1. [Open Gemini Notebook](https://notebook.google.com/) using an account and device permitted for your situation. Work or school access can depend on your organization.
2. Create a notebook and add your sources. Supported source types include PDFs, web pages and Google documents. Check that the imported text is what you expected. [Adding sources](https://support.google.com/gemininotebook/answer/16215270?hl=en)
3. Ask one question below. Open a citation and see whether the source really supports the answer. Try a follow-up in your own words.

## Help me get my bearings

> I'm new to this topic. Using these sources, explain the five things I should understand first. Keep the jargon to a minimum, cite the relevant passages, and flag anything the sources don't answer.

## Help me untangle a disagreement

> These sources seem to say different things. Where do they agree, where do they differ, and could the difference come from their dates or intended audiences? Show me the passages. Don't smooth over a real disagreement.

## Help me teach it back

> I have five minutes to explain this to someone new. Give me a short outline, one everyday analogy, and three questions to check understanding. Separate what the sources say from your suggested teaching ideas.

An Audio Overview can offer another way into the material; a mind map can help you see the connections. Both are generated interpretations. Listen or browse, then return to the sources for anything you plan to rely on.

## A little judgment travels well

Check source dates, missing material and claims that seem too tidy. Imported material may be a copy rather than a live view; recheck the original when freshness matters. A citation makes checking easier; it does not make an answer automatically correct.

Use your organization's approved access route for work. Account editions have different terms and data protections; an organizational email address alone does not establish which applies.
